AI顶级初创公司很少发表研究成果

原题：AI's top startups are barely publishing their research

Today’s biggest artificial intelligence (AI) startups make no shortage of bold promises. Their technologies, some boast, will revolutionize software development, drug discovery, and scientific research.

Yet a new preprint posted on 16 July on bioRxiv suggests many of these firms barely participate in one of science’s most fundamental practices: publicly documenting discoveries in scientific literature so other researchers can evaluate and build on them. More than half of AI unicorns—private companies valued at more than $1 billion—have never played a leading role in publishing a scientific paper or preprint, according to the new analysis. Collectively, they accounted for just one in every 1000 AI papers published in 2025.

“For a field that is supposedly reshaping science and is so advanced in terms of scientific potential, not having any scientific documentation seems like a very weird paradox,” says paper co-author John Ioannidis, a metascientist at Stanford University. “How can you judge that what they say is real, validated, and reproducible?” The scarcity of publications, others say, also makes it harder to assess AI’s social impacts, including energy use and safety.

But University of Alberta AI ethicist Mohamed Abdalla says the findings reflect the incentives facing commercial AI developers, rather than solely a failure to uphold scientific norms. “It’s not the company’s job to advance science, right?” he says. “The company’s job is to advance money.”

Ioannidis has long studied how unicorns, particularly in biotech, engage with the scientific literature. (In 2015, he was the first to publicly scrutinize the lack of peer-reviewed studies produced by Theranos, the blood testing startup that proved to be based on fraudulent data.) He wondered whether AI unicorns would show similar patterns.

To find out, he and his team first identified all 317 unicorn AI companies that have existed from 1998 to 2025. Then, they searched for publications affiliated with these startups—including journal articles, conference papers, reviews, and preprints. They selected those where a company researcher played a leading role as a first or last author, indicating the startup had made a substantial contribution to the work. The final data set included 2077 final publications, comprising 1389 peer-reviewed papers and 688 preprints.

More than half of the startups had never produced a single qualifying paper, the analysis revealed. Scientific influence proved even more concentrated, with the top 5% of firms accounting for greater than 90% of all citations. OpenAI alone was responsible for nearly 40% of all citations in the data set, followed by the Chinese computer vision company Megvii and the platform Hugging Face. And even at the most prolific companies, much of the output came from the same small group of repeat authors. For example, despite OpenAI employing roughly 4500 people, only eight researchers had authored five or more qualifying papers.

The findings are unsurprising to some AI researchers given how the industry is structured. For example, unlike the pharmaceutical industry, where published discoveries can be protected by patents, AI companies have learned they often gain little from publicly disclosing technical advances, says Nur Ahmed, an AI researcher at the University of Arkansas. Google’s landmark 2017 paper on the transformer—the architecture that underpins today’s large language models—has become a classic cautionary example, Abdalla adds. Although Google patented aspects of the technology, “I don’t think anybody’s paying Google for that,” he says.

Startups also operate on much faster timelines than academia, where peer review can lumber on for months or even years. That’s why many AI companies have embraced what Avijit Ghosh, an AI policy researcher at Hugging Face, calls the “blogification” of research: announcing new models and releasing code or data sets through blog posts and technical reports rather than scientific journals. The new analysis didn’t track those outputs, he points out.

For Ghosh, the debate shouldn’t center on publishing in journals versus blogs. What matters is whether companies are releasing enough code, data sets, or model weights (the numbers that determine how a model interprets and responds to a prompt) for others to independently verify and build on their work, he says.

The preprint also found that firms based in China consistently published more papers than their counterparts based in the United States. Whereas leading U.S. frontier labs have increasingly kept the details of their most capable models secret or “closed sourced,” leading Chinese companies have embraced “open-source” models. Moonshot AI, one of the Chinese startups included in the study, recently unveiled Kimi K3—one of the strongest open models to date—and publicly released its model weights through Hugging Face today.

But whether models are open or closed, the rapid pace toward increasingly powerful generalist AI worries Emma Pierson, a computer scientist at the University of California, Berkeley. She argues AI research—whether published freely or kept secret—risks accelerating models that pose serious societal and safety concerns, including supercharging cyberattacks. “If we were racing forward on cancer-curing AI, I would be like, ’Fantastic, full steam ahead,’” she says. “But that’s not what we’re racing toward, right?”

当今最大的人工智能（AI）初创公司不乏大胆的承诺。他们中的一些人吹嘘，其技术将彻底改变软件开发、药物发现和科学研究。

然而，7月16日发布在bioRxiv上的一篇新预印本论文表明，这些公司中的许多几乎没有参与科学界最基本实践之一：在科学文献中公开记录发现，以便其他研究人员能够评估并在此基础上继续研究。根据这项新分析，超过一半的AI独角兽（估值超过10亿美元的私营公司）从未在发表科学论文或预印本中扮演过主导角色。总体而言，它们在2025年发表的每1000篇AI论文中仅占一篇。

“对于一个据称正在重塑科学、在科学潜力方面如此先进的领域，没有任何科学记录似乎是一个非常奇怪的悖论，”论文合著者、斯坦福大学的元科学家John Ioannidis说。“你怎么能判断他们所说的东西是真实、经过验证且可复现的呢？”其他人说，出版物稀缺也使得评估AI的社会影响（包括能源使用和安全性）变得更加困难。

但阿尔伯塔大学的AI伦理学家Mohamed Abdalla表示，这些发现反映了商业AI开发者面临的激励措施，而不仅仅是未能坚持科学规范。“公司的职责不是推进科学，对吧？”他说。“公司的职责是赚钱。”

Ioannidis长期以来一直研究独角兽公司（尤其是在生物技术领域）如何与科学文献互动。（2015年，他首次公开审查了血液检测初创公司Theranos缺乏同行评审研究的问题，该公司后来被证明是基于欺诈数据。）他想知道AI独角兽是否会表现出类似的模式。

为了找出答案，他和他的团队首先确定了从1998年到2025年间存在的所有317家AI独角兽公司。然后，他们搜索了与这些初创公司相关的出版物——包括期刊文章、会议论文、综述和预印本。他们选择了公司研究人员作为第一作者或最后作者发挥主导作用的论文，这表明初创公司对该工作做出了实质性贡献。最终数据集包含2077篇最终出版物，其中包括1389篇同行评审论文和688篇预印本。

分析显示，超过一半的初创公司从未发表过任何符合标准的论文。科学影响力则更为集中，排名前5%的公司占据了所有引用量的90%以上。仅OpenAI一家就占数据集中总引用量的近40%，其次是中国的计算机视觉公司Megvii和平台Hugging Face。即使在成果最丰硕的公司，大部分产出也来自同一小群重复发表的研究人员。例如，尽管OpenAI雇佣了约4500人，但只有八名研究人员发表了五篇或更多符合标准的论文。

对于一些AI研究人员来说，这些发现并不令人意外，因为行业结构就是如此。阿肯色大学的AI研究员Nur Ahmed表示，例如，与制药行业不同（在该行业，已发表的研究发现可以通过专利保护），AI公司已经意识到，公开披露技术进步往往收益甚微。Abdalla补充说，谷歌2017年关于transformer的里程碑式论文——这一架构支撑着当今的大型语言模型——已成为一个经典的警示案例。尽管谷歌为该技术的某些方面申请了专利，但他说：“我不认为有人会因此向谷歌付费。”

初创公司的运营节奏也比学术界快得多，而学术界的同行评审可能持续数月甚至数年。这就是为什么许多AI公司接受了Hugging Face的AI政策研究员Avijit Ghosh所谓的“研究博客化”：通过博客文章和技术报告而非科学期刊来宣布新模型、发布代码或数据集。他指出，新的分析并未追踪这些产出。

对Ghosh而言，争论的焦点不应是期刊发表与博客发布的对立。重要的是，公司是否发布了足够的代码、数据集或模型权重（决定模型如何解读和响应用户提示的数值），以便他人能够独立验证并在此基础上进行开发，他说。

该预印本研究还发现，中国公司的论文发表量持续超过其美国同行。而美国领先的前沿实验室越来越倾向于将其最强大模型的细节保密或“闭源”，领先的中国公司则拥抱“开源”模型。研究中所包含的中国初创公司之一Moonshot AI最近发布了Kimi K3——迄今为止最强大的开放模型之一，并于今天通过Hugging Face公开了其模型权重。

但无论模型是开源还是闭源，通用人工智能能力快速提升的趋势都让加州大学伯克利分校的计算机科学家艾玛·皮尔森（Emma Pierson）感到担忧。她认为，人工智能研究——无论公开还是保密——都可能加速发展出带来严重社会和安全风险的模型，包括加剧网络攻击。“如果我们是在竞相研发治愈癌症的人工智能，我会说：‘太棒了，全速前进！’”她说，“但我们现在竞相发展的可不是这个，对吧？”
